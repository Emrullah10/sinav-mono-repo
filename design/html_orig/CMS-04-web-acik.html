Özgün Okuma Metni (Literata Serif)

Although the initial architectural excavations in the Indus Valley indicated a strictly egalitarian social structure devoid of monumental monarchical palaces, subsequent stratigraphical analyses have revealed that sophisticated administrative hierarchies did in fact govern municipal affairs. The rigorous uniformity observed in urban planning—most notably seen in standardized brick proportions across distant trade outposts—suggests a centralized bureaucratic oversight rather than spontaneous coordination among isolated village communes.

Moreover, recent bioarchaeological studies conducted on skeletal remains within mature Harappan settlements corroborate that nutritional access and domestic spatial distribution exhibited profound socioeconomic disparities. Consequently, contemporary archaeologists increasingly discard the romantic notion of an idyllic proto-democracy, acknowledging that elite factions wielded civic authority through ideological consensus and monopolized mercantilism instead of overt military coercion.
